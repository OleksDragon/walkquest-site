A bigger home for every WalkQuest journey

The new WalkQuest website is more than a landing page: it is now a multilingual field guide for understanding the game before your first step.

October 1, 2026•6 min read

A walking game can be simple to play and still contain a whole world of details. WalkQuest connects steps, routes, cities, transfers, passport stamps, achievements, and Premium options. The redesigned website brings those pieces together in one calm place.

The goal is practical: a visitor should understand what WalkQuest does in less than a minute, while an active traveller can find a deeper answer without searching through menus.

Sixteen languages, one shared journey

Every main page and every article follows the language selected on the site. On a first visit, WalkQuest checks the browser language; if it is supported, the page opens in that language automatically. Otherwise, English is used.

The choice is saved on the device and follows you from the game guide to routes, Premium, privacy, and the journal. Arabic also changes the complete reading direction to right-to-left.

Why this matters

A player arriving directly from Google Play can read the explanation, privacy policy, and support material in a familiar language without creating an account.

A clearer path from curiosity to the first walk

The website now separates quick answers from detailed guidance. The home page explains the promise; the game guide shows the controls; the route atlas helps choose a destination; the Premium page explains purchases without hiding the free experience.

🗺️

Game guide

See how steps move the marker, how cities unlock, and what happens at a ferry, train, or flight gate.

🧭

Route atlas

Compare short adventures with epic journeys using real distances, stops, difficulty, and transfer counts.

📔

Journal

Read focused stories about releases, destinations, and practical setup instead of vague marketing announcements.

What has not changed

WalkQuest still does not require an account. Step access remains read-only and permission-based. Your real movement becomes progress on the route you choose, while the website remains a lightweight static site with no sign-in wall.

The website should feel like the first page of a travel journal: useful before the journey and worth returning to after it begins.

What comes next

Future notes will explain meaningful changes in plain language: what changed, why it changed, and whether it affects existing progress. Route spotlights and practical guides will continue alongside them.
