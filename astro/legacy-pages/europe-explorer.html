WalkQuest Europe Explorer: 1,240 km from London to Prague

Twelve city stops, six countries, and one free route designed to turn an ordinary walking habit into a continental adventure.

Europe Explorer is the route we recommend when somebody asks, “Where should I begin?” It is long enough to feel like a real expedition, varied enough to keep the map interesting, and available without a Premium purchase.

The journey begins in London and moves east toward Prague. Along the way, each reached city becomes more than a dot: it opens a story, local facts, landmarks, food, map links, and a new passport stamp.

Why it works as a first route

The Easy difficulty is not about making the kilometres disappear. It means the route has a friendly rhythm and an understandable goal. At roughly 1,250 steps per virtual kilometre before bonuses, even a modest daily walk produces visible progress.

A useful pace

Do not chase the finish line on day one. Pick a daily walking target you can repeat, then let the cities arrive as rewards for consistency.

The pleasure is between the famous landmarks

London, Paris, alpine scenery, historic streets, and Prague provide the big postcard moments. The quieter transitions matter too: watching the marker leave one city, cross a border, and approach the next destination gives an ordinary week a sense of direction.

When a stop unlocks, pause for a minute. Read the short introduction, open a landmark, look at Street View when it is available, and add the stamp to your passport. That small ritual is what turns distance into a journey.

London blends royal landmarks, riverside walks, busy markets, and quiet museum corners.

Interesting fact

Dover

United Kingdom

Dover welcomes travelers with chalk cliffs, a historic harbor, and views across the English Channel.

Interesting fact

Dover Castle contains tunnels used from the Napoleonic era through the Second World War.

Calais

France

Calais combines a busy Channel port with a grand belfry, maritime history, and a strong lace-making tradition.

Interesting fact

The belfry of Calais Town Hall forms part of a UNESCO-listed group of belfries in France and Belgium.

Paris

France

Paris is a city of grand boulevards, art-filled museums, glowing bridges, and cafe terraces.

Interesting fact

The Eiffel Tower was originally built for the 1889 World’s Fair.

Lille

France

Lille blends colorful Flemish architecture, major art collections, and lively cafe-filled squares.

Interesting fact

Lille's Vieille Bourse was built in the seventeenth century as a group of twenty-four merchants' houses around a courtyard.

Brussels

Belgium

Brussels mixes elegant squares, comic murals, European institutions, and sweet chocolate stops.

Interesting fact

The Grand Place is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Antwerp

Belgium

Antwerp is a Scheldt River city of monumental guild houses, fashion, art, and a vast working port.

Interesting fact

The world's first purpose-built commodity exchange opened in Antwerp in 1531.

Rotterdam

Netherlands

Rotterdam pairs experimental modern architecture with river views and one of the world's largest ports.

Interesting fact

Rotterdam rebuilt its center with a deliberately modern identity after extensive destruction in 1940.

Amsterdam

Netherlands

Amsterdam is a canal city of bicycles, narrow houses, museums, and soft reflections.

Interesting fact

The city has more than 100 kilometers of canals.

Hannover offers royal gardens, a walkable old center, and generous green spaces along the route east.

Interesting fact

When the road becomes a ferry, train, or flight

At a special transfer gate, walking progress pauses at the shore or terminal. Choose how to start the crossing.

  • Travel points — Spend the number of points shown on the gate and wait for the route’s travel time.
  • Premium — The crossing is included, costs no travel points, and uses a one-day travel time.

Make the route fit your real life

  1. Choose a repeatable baseline.A twenty-minute walk you actually take is more valuable than an ambitious plan you abandon.
  2. Check the next city, not the final kilometre.Short targets make a 1,240 km route feel inviting instead of overwhelming.
  3. Use the story as the reward.Open the city card after arrival and give yourself time to explore what you unlocked.

What happens at the finish

Completing Europe Explorer gives you a finished route, a record of every reached city, and the confidence to choose a more demanding world next. Italy, Japan, Iceland, Greek islands, Route 66, and global journeys are waiting when you want a different rhythm.

The best route is not the longest one. It is the route that makes tomorrow’s walk feel worth taking.
